# Slide 2 — Problem Statement

Before explaining our solution, I would like to explain the problem we are trying to solve.

A Medical Representative, or MR, usually has to visit multiple doctors within a particular territory.

The difficulty is that every doctor may have different requirements.

For example, one doctor may have higher visit priority, another doctor may only be available during a particular time window, while another doctor may be geographically far away.

In many planning situations, the daily visit schedule can depend heavily on manual planning, previous experience or familiar routes.

This can lead to several problems such as repeated travel, inefficient routes, difficulty in prioritizing doctors, and limited visibility for managers.

So our main problem statement is:

**“How can AI and optimization techniques be used to automatically prioritize doctor visits and generate an efficient daily visit schedule for a Medical Representative?”**

This is the core problem our project attempts to solve.

---

# Slide 3 — Motivation

Now, why did we select this problem?

Let us consider a simple example.

Suppose one Medical Representative has ten doctors to potentially visit in a day.

Doctor A and Doctor C may have high priority.

Doctor D may only be available after 4 PM.

Doctor E may be 12 kilometres away from the base.

And Doctor F may be only one kilometre away from Doctor A.

If we manually create the route, we may consider only one or two of these factors.

But the actual planning problem requires us to consider **priority, availability, distance, travel time and visit duration together**.

This is where our motivation comes from.

We want to combine three technologies:

**Artificial Intelligence for visit prioritization, appointment constraints for scheduling, and route optimization for deciding the best sequence of visits.**

Therefore, instead of depending only on experience or habit, our system attempts to make the planning process **data-driven and systematic**.

There is also research and industrial evidence showing that pharmaceutical sales-force scheduling and routing are genuine optimization problems, which further motivated us to work on this domain.

# Slide 4 — Aim and Objectives

Our aim is:

**ML prediction → appointment constraints → route optimization → web application → manager analytics.**

So our project is not claiming that route optimization or field-force management is a completely new problem.

Instead, our contribution is the **integration of predictive visit prioritization and constraint-based route optimization into a single domain-specific web platform.**

# Slide 6 — Abstract / System Overview

Now I will briefly explain the complete system.

Our system starts with historical doctor and visit-related data.

This data is preprocessed and given to a Random Forest model.

The model produces a priority score for each planned doctor visit.

For example:

Doctor A may receive a priority score of 0.91, while Doctor B may receive 0.62.

This priority information is then combined with appointment constraints such as:

* doctor availability,
* visit duration,
* MR working hours,
* and geographical information.

These constraints are given to the Google OR-Tools optimization engine.

The optimizer then solves a Vehicle Routing Problem with Time Windows and generates an appropriate sequence of visits.

Finally, the optimized plan is displayed through the web dashboard.

So the complete flow is:

**Historical Data → Preprocessing → ML Priority Prediction → Priority Score → Appointment Constraints → Route Optimization → Daily MR Plan → Dashboard and Reports.**

This is the central workflow of our project.

# Slide 8 — Proposed System

Now I will explain how the proposed system will actually work.

The first layer is historical data.

This may contain doctor information, visit history, appointment information and other operational features.

Next comes data preprocessing and feature engineering.

The processed information is provided to the machine-learning model.

Our primary model is Random Forest, which will generate a visit-priority score.

After that, we apply appointment constraints.

For example:

A doctor may only be available from 10 AM to 12 PM.

The MR may have working hours from 9 AM to 6 PM.

Each doctor may require approximately a particular amount of visit time.

All these constraints are considered by the optimization engine.

Google OR-Tools will then generate the optimized sequence.

Finally, the Medical Representative receives a daily visit plan through the dashboard.

# Slide 10 — Architecture

Now coming to the system architecture.

At the top, we have three types of users:

**Medical Representative, Doctor and Manager.**

They interact with the system through the React.js frontend.

The frontend communicates with the FastAPI backend through REST APIs.

The backend handles authentication, appointment management, business logic and communication with the AI and optimization modules.

MySQL stores doctors, Medical Representatives, appointments and visit history.

The Random Forest engine is responsible for predicting the visit-priority score.

The Google OR-Tools engine then performs the route optimization using the priority and operational constraints.

Finally, the optimized information is displayed through the manager dashboard and analytics module.

So technically, the important point is:

**The ML model makes the priority prediction, while OR-Tools solves the scheduling and routing problem.**

They are two different components working together.
